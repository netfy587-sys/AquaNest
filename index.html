What This Guide Is All About

This website helps beginners understand home aquariums. It explains how a tank works, how to look after fish and plants, and how to make choices that keep animals healthy and your tank easy to manage.

Why keep an aquarium?

A well-kept tank is calming to watch and teaches patience, observation and responsibility. It can also be a small living ecosystem in your home. Fish are living animals though, so the hobby works best when you research each species and plan carefully before you buy.

The nitrogen cycle, simply explained

Fish produce waste, and waste breaks down into ammonia, which is harmful in even small amounts. Helpful bacteria in the filter turn ammonia into nitrite, and then into the much less harmful nitrate. Building up these bacteria is called cycling the tank, and it usually takes several weeks.

This is why a brand-new tank should run for a while before fish are added. A basic test kit lets you check that ammonia and nitrite read zero before you introduce your first residents.

Freshwater or saltwater?

Freshwater tanks are generally simpler and more forgiving, which makes them the usual choice for beginners. Saltwater tanks can be beautiful, but they need more equipment, tighter control of water chemistry and more experience.

Tank size and equipment

A larger tank is usually easier to keep stable, because changes in temperature and water quality happen more slowly. Always check how big a fish grows as an adult before you buy. Most tanks need a filter, a heater for tropical species, lighting, a thermometer, a substrate such as gravel or sand, and a water conditioner that removes chlorine from tap water.

Feeding and routine care

Feed small amounts once or twice a day, and remove food that is not eaten, because leftovers pollute the water. Change part of the water regularly, clean the filter gently in used tank water rather than tap water and watch your fish daily. Changes in behaviour or appearance are often the first sign of a problem.

Choosing fish that get along

Not all fish live well together. Some are peaceful, some are territorial and some grow large enough to eat smaller tank mates. Ask an experienced aquarium shop, read species guides and add fish a few at a time, so the filter can adjust.

Quick Facts

  • Many tropical fish are kept at roughly 24 to 27 degrees Celsius
  • Ammonia and nitrite should both read zero in a settled tank
  • Tap water needs a conditioner to remove chlorine
  • Bigger tanks are usually easier for beginners
  • Never release pet fish or plants into local waters

Find Your Best Match

The best aquarium is the one that suits your space, time and budget. AquaNest helps you think it through before you buy a single fish.

  • Decide how much space you can offer
  • Research each fish: size, temperament and needs
  • Choose species that share similar water conditions
  • Start small and add fish slowly
  • Plan a regular, simple care routine

Ready to Discover More?

Healthy water is the secret of every happy tank. A few simple habits matter more than expensive gear.

  • Let the tank settle before adding any fish
  • Test the water regularly with a basic kit
  • Change a portion of the water on a steady schedule
  • Do not overfeed: small portions are enough
  • Never release pet fish into rivers or the sea

Questions People Ask

Short answers to common questions, so you can understand the basics quickly.

How long should I wait before adding fish?

Usually several weeks, while the tank cycles. Test the water and add fish only when ammonia and nitrite read zero.

Why did my new tank turn cloudy?

Cloudiness in a new tank is common and is often a harmless bloom of bacteria. It usually clears by itself if you avoid overfeeding and keep the filter running.

How often should I feed my fish?

Small portions once or twice a day are enough for most species. Only give what they finish in a couple of minutes.

Can I use tap water in my tank?

Usually yes, after treating it with a water conditioner that removes chlorine and similar chemicals. Ask your local aquarium shop about your water supply.
